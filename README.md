# Model Amicus Brief — Danco Laboratories, LLC v. Louisiana / GenBioPro, Inc. v. Louisiana

## Subtitle

Medication Abortion, Federalism, and Constitutional Continuity Across State and Federal Systems

## What is this?

This is a public model amicus brief written as if submitted in support of Applicants Danco Laboratories, LLC, and GenBioPro, Inc., in their Supreme Court emergency applications concerning the May 1, 2026 order of the United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit.

It has not been filed in any court. It is not legal advice. A professional lawyer would need to adapt, verify, format, and file any actual brief.

## Why should I care?

The brief addresses a federalism problem raised by emergency litigation over access to mifepristone: when may one State’s asserted injury justify interim relief that affects a national medication-access framework before merits review is complete?

## What is the claim?

The brief argues that state authority remains real, but bounded. One State should not be able to use emergency federal litigation to project its policy into a national medication-access framework, disrupt reliance interests, and burden patients, providers, manufacturers, pharmacies, and federal regulators before the Supreme Court has completed review.

The brief names this danger “boundary capture”: the use of a legitimate jurisdictional boundary as a lever to control persons, actors, or systems beyond the boundary’s lawful reach.

## What is this not?

This is not a filed brief, legal advice, a substitute for Supreme Court briefing rules, or a replacement for standing, preemption, administrative-law, remedial, emergency-relief, FDA-authority, or state-police-power doctrine. It does not ask the Court to decide every question concerning abortion law or medication abortion.

## How was this done?

The brief translates Constitutional Physiology, Structural Flow, and Federalism-as-scale-discipline into court-facing language. It uses the frame of state authority, federal continuity, patient access, institutional reliance, public interest, and emergency-relief discipline rather than internal theory vocabulary.
